3-Wheel Omni Robot

holonomic drive 3 omniwheels rotary encoders ROS Arduino Mega Raspberry Pi SLAM ultrasonic LIDAR

A holonomic mobile robot platform built around three omnidirectional wheels arranged 120° apart, each driven by a gearbox motor with a rotary encoder. The three-wheel configuration gives the robot three degrees of freedom on the plane — it can move in any direction (x/y) and rotate (θ) simultaneously, without needing to turn first.

The mechanical structure uses off-the-shelf Matrix kit aluminum extrusions. The electronics stack is split across two boards: an Arduino Mega handles the low-level firmware — motor drivers, encoder reading, closed-loop speed control — while a Raspberry Pi runs a full ROS environment on top. The two communicate over serial, with ROS sending velocity commands down to the Arduino.

The sensing system is a custom ultrasonic LIDAR substitute. Five ultrasonic sensors are mounted on a platform at the top of the robot; a servo motor sweeps the platform back and forth to scan the surrounding area. The sensor readings are processed on the Raspberry Pi and published as a point cloud in ROS — enough data for the standard ROS navigation stack to do SLAM (simultaneous localization and mapping) and full autonomous motion planning.

This approach trades millimeter accuracy for cost: a real spinning LIDAR is hundreds to thousands of dollars. The spinning ultrasonic head is under $10 in parts.
