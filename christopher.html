Meet Christopher

PIR sensor single DC motor JK flip-flop mechanical design

Christopher is a ceiling-mounted skeleton that drops silently over unsuspecting people's heads, then retracts and waits in ambush for the next victim.

The mechanism is triggered by a PIR motion sensor. A single DC motor spools Christopher up and holds him under tension. When the sensor fires, the mechanism releases Christopher — not unwinds, releases — so the drop is instant and jarring. After the scare, reversing the motor direction retracts him back up and resets the ambush automatically.

The clever part is that this full multi-state cycle (hold → release → retract → hold) is driven by a single motor, using only direction reversal. No servos. No complex circuitry. The entire electronics package reduces to a cheap motor driver IC and a JK flip-flop.

The whole assembly is snap-fit. Not a single screw was used.

Christopher claimed a lot of victims at SFU on Halloween 2025.
