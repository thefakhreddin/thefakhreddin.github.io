Research & Publications

IEEE Sensors Journal · 2025 · with A. Ali, J. Ziegler, J. Walmsley, B. Bahreyni

Inductive Sensors for Continuous Bath and Metal Level Measurement in Harsh Aluminum Smelting Environments

Aluminum smelting cells operate at ~960 °C with large DC currents and corrosive molten cryolite — a brutal environment that has historically defeated every in-situ sensor. The current industry standard is still a technician poking a dipstick into the pot. This paper proposes two non-contact inductive sensors based on eddy-current principles that measure cryolite bath thickness and molten aluminum pool depth without touching the melt. A single-inductor design establishes the core mechanism; a differential induction-balance configuration improves resolution. Both are validated analytically, in COMSOL simulation, and in furnace experiments with real cryolite, demonstrating clear depth discrimination — a first step toward closing the loop on bath geometry control.

IEEE Sensors Conference (Outstanding Lecture Award) · Oct 2025 · with A. Ali, B. Bahreyni

Real-Time Monitoring of Molten Cryolite Composition Using a Resonant Capacitive Sensor

Companion work to the inductive level sensor above: instead of measuring where the molten aluminum is, this paper targets what the bath is made of. Aluminum fluoride concentration drifts during smelting and must be kept in a tight range to control liquidus temperature and conductivity — but measuring it in real time has been an open problem. The solution here is a ceramic-coated passive LC resonator probe that partially submerges in the bath. Changes in bath conductivity detune the resonator's quality factor in a predictable way, letting you read out AlF₃ concentration from an impedance measurement. Validated at multiple concentrations in molten cryolite. This talk was awarded Outstanding Lecture at the conference among 400+ accepted works.

Neural Processing Letters · Jan 2025 · with Z. Nilforoushan, S. L. Mirtaheri

LPM-Net: A Data-Driven Resource-Efficient Predictive Motion Planner for Mobile Robots

Model predictive control (MPC) makes smooth, collision-aware robot paths but is computationally heavy — a problem on small embedded hardware. LPM-Net replaces the MPC solver with a CNN + LSTM network trained via imitation learning to mimic the planner's outputs while satisfying the same kinodynamic constraints. Once trained, inference runs on GPU/TPU-enabled SoCs at roughly half the processing power of the conventional MPC, with a marginal increase in goal-reaching hesitation. Packaged as a ROS-compatible local planner and open-sourced on GitHub.

IEEE Transactions on Green Communications and Networking · Jun 2022 · with Y. Mafi, S. A. Hosseini, A. Azari, M. Masoudi, M. Vaezi

Ultra-Low-Power IoT Communications: A Novel Address Decoding Approach for Wake-Up Receivers

A key challenge in massive IoT deployments is keeping billions of battery-powered devices reachable without draining them. Wake-up receivers (WuR) solve this by letting a device hibernate until the network pages its unique address — but the address decoder itself burns power continuously. This paper proposes a new decoder architecture that cuts that consumption to 60 nW, well below the state-of-the-art, while maintaining reliable decoding. System-level analysis identifies the operating regimes where WuR-based hibernation meaningfully extends battery life, making the case for its integration into 5G/6G IoT infrastructure.
